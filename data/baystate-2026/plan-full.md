## How to use this plan

- Daily decision: read the traffic light first. Complete only the scheduled work. Good days are not permission to add mileage; yellow days are permission to protect the next week.
- Use RPE as the universal control. Pace and heart rate are secondary to effort, symptoms, heat, hills, surface, and recovery.
- Fill the injury checkpoint before, during, immediately after, and the next morning. The next-morning value controls progression.
- Every planned distance is a ceiling. The acceptable weekly range is a normal part of the plan, especially on symptom-gated long runs.
- Cycling substitutes for running only when symptom-free and useful; it never repays missed mileage. Hard cycling is removed as run quality grows.
- This plan assumes ongoing clinician/PT involvement for recurrent symptoms and a biomechanics assessment used as information, not as proof of a single cause.

**Important medical boundary:** Probable patellofemoral pain, the prior plantar-region issue, and the residual right-foot sensation are not diagnosed here. Stop and seek clinical reassessment for sharp/focal pain, swelling, altered gait, pain in normal walking or stairs, progressive symptoms, or elevation beyond 24 hours. Mild pain does not automatically mean damage; steadily worsening pain is not normalized.

**Plan logic in one sentence:** Four separated easy runs -> five-day frequency -> controlled threshold -> full-distance easy tolerance -> broken HM-specific work -> reduced-volume taper -> evidence-based A/B/C race choice.

## Pain and load traffic light

| Light | Signals | Action |
|---|---|---|
| GREEN | Pain 0-2/10; stable or improves; normal gait, stairs, and walking; no next-morning increase; foot and calf at baseline. | Continue only as scheduled. Progress at the next planned step; never add bonus distance. |
| YELLOW | Pain around 3/10; mild increase; morning stiffness above baseline; foot feels more unusual; calf soreness rises; readiness/energy deteriorates; mechanics feel altered. | Do not progress. Repeat or reduce; replace quality with easy running or cycling; hold weekly volume; reassess next morning. |
| RED | Pain >3/10; progressive pain; altered gait; pain walking/stairs; symptoms elevated >24 h; swelling; sharp/focal pain; significant foot or calf worsening. | Stop. Remove the next hard workout, return to prior tolerable load, use symptom-free cycling only if appropriate, and consider PT/medical reassessment. |

**Return-protocol soreness rule retained:**
- Sore during warm-up: take two days off and drop one prior tolerable step.
- Sore during the workout: take one day off and drop one step.
- Sore after the workout only: hold at the same load; do not advance.

## Why this design fits the evidence

- Current best practice for patellofemoral pain centers education plus exercise therapy, especially knee-targeted and hip-targeted strengthening, modified to irritability and the person's presentation. Supporting interventions such as taping or prefabricated foot orthoses may help selected people, but are not universal fixes [1,2].
- Load is progressed by response rather than an arbitrary calendar. Evidence does not validate a universal weekly 10% rule, while a large 2025 cohort found that unusually large single-session distance jumps were associated with running injury risk [4,5]. This plan therefore watches the longest single run, uses cutbacks, and distributes volume across frequency.
- Gait retraining is a supporting tool, not a diagnosis. A 10% step-rate intervention improved selected mechanics and outcomes in a small PFP runner study, but that does not prove a universal cadence or require a forefoot strike [3]. Brief strides and +3-5% cadence samples are used only under green lights.
- Most running remains low intensity; threshold and race-specific work are layered later. Reviews generally favor pyramidal/polarized distributions over threshold-heavy programs [7]. Heavy or combined strength training can improve running economy, but this athlete already lifts frequently, so the program prioritizes consistency, subfailure sets, and sequencing over maximal novelty [8,9].
- The taper reduces volume while preserving brief intensity and rhythm. Meta-analysis supports meaningful volume reduction while maintaining intensity and, where practical, frequency [6].

**Clinical interpretation:** This evidence shapes guardrails; it cannot predict an individual's flare. The day-to-day symptom response remains the governing signal.

**HR rule:** The recorded maximum of ~202 bpm makes broad zones possible, but HR lags, drifts with heat, and cycling HR is lower. Use HR as a post-session sense check. Never speed up to hit a number; slow down when RPE, breathing, or mechanics exceed the session purpose.

A natural cadence near 170-180 may emerge at faster paces, but no universal target is imposed. Never combine an aggressive gait change with a volume jump, and do not force a forefoot strike.

## Strength and concurrent-training system

**Weekly pattern:** Three upper sessions and two lower sessions are retained through most of the block. Lower volume shrinks in peak/taper weeks. Run quality comes first when paired on the same day; separate sessions by about six hours where practical [9].

| Template | Content |
|---|---|
| Lower A | Bulgarian split squat 3x6/side; leg extension 3x8-12; hamstring curl 3x8-12; standing calf raise 3x8-12; hip abduction 2x12-15. RPE 7; no failure. |
| Lower B | Leg press or squat variation 3x6-8; controlled step-down 3x8/side; bent-knee soleus raise 3x10-15; hamstring curl 2x10-12; foot doming + toe yoga 2x8 each. RPE 6-7. |
| Lower maintenance | Bulgarian split squat 2x5/side; leg extension 2x8; hamstring curl 2x8; calf + soleus raise 2x10 each; hip abduction 2x12. RPE 6, stop very fresh. |
| Upper A | Bench press 3x5-8; chest-supported row 3x6-10; incline DB press 2x8-12; pulldown 2x8-12; optional arms 2x10-15. RPE 7, 2-3 reps in reserve. |
| Upper B | Overhead press 3x5-8; pull-up/pulldown 3x6-10; cable row 2x8-12; lateral raise 2x12-15; optional arms 2x10-15. RPE 7. |
| Upper C | Incline press 3x6-10; one-arm cable row 3x8-12; machine press 2x8-12; rear-delt fly 2x12-15; arms 2x10-15. RPE 6-7. |
| Mobility / foot | 8-10 min: ankle rocks 2x10/side, supported step-down 2x8/side, lateral band walk 2x10/side, foot doming 2x8, single-leg balance 2x30 s. Symptom-free range. |

**Strength rules:**
- RPE 6-8, normally 2-3 reps in reserve; no frequent failure.
- No mandatory Romanian deadlifts. Use hamstring curls and preferred squat/press/split-squat patterns.
- Painful knee flexion is modified by range, load, tempo, or exercise selection; do not grind through symptoms.
- No heavy lower session immediately before threshold, HM-specific work, or the long run.
- If soreness changes running mechanics, reduce strength volume before reducing key running quality.

## Fueling, hydration, and recovery

The goal is adequate energy availability, stable recovery, and enough carbohydrate to support running, cycling, lifting, and university life - not restrictive eating. Exact calories are not prescribed without height, intake, weight trend, and expenditure data [10].

| Session | Before / during | After |
|---|---|---|
| Easy <60 min | Fuel based on meal timing; water to thirst; usually no during-run carbohydrate. | Normal meal; include protein and carbohydrate. |
| Quality 45-75 min | Carbohydrate snack/meal 1-3 h before; water; caffeine only if tested. | Carbohydrate + ~25-35 g protein within a practical meal window. |
| Long 75-120 min | Practice 30-60 g carbohydrate/h; fluid roughly 400-750 mL/h by sweat and weather; sodium individualized. | Recovery meal within 1-2 h; continue fluids and normal salt intake. |
| Race | Carbohydrate-forward previous day; familiar breakfast 2.5-3.5 h before; gel plan tested in long runs. | Fluids, carbohydrate, protein, and a normal meal; no compensation/restriction. |

**Readiness signals:** Watch sleep, resting HR relative to personal baseline, HRV trend, energy, stress, appetite, muscle soreness, and Garmin readiness. No single metric vetoes training; two or more negative trends plus symptoms justify a hold or reduction.

## Race morning, fueling, and contingencies

**Race morning timeline:**
- ~4:30-5:15 AM: wake based on travel; familiar carbohydrate-rich breakfast 2.5-3.5 h before start; normal caffeine only if tested.
- By ~6:30 AM: arrive/park early enough to avoid closure stress; bib and gear check well before lines grow.
- 7:20-7:25 AM: begin 10-12 min easy warm-up, mobility drills, and 4x20 s strides; finish 8-10 min before start; keep disposable layer on.
- ~7:50 AM: optional tested gel with small water. Start calm on the correct half-marathon side.
- Race: likely one gel around 35-45 min; sip water/Gatorade according to practice. Do not rely on an unverified on-course gel for the half.

**Contingencies:**
- Heat/humidity: Downgrade from pace to RPE; start 5-10 s/km slower or more; hydrate; abandon A if heat strain rises.
- Wind: Run effort, use groups without surging, accept slower exposed splits.
- Rain/cold: Dry socks/anti-chafe, disposable layer, secure footing on bridges/turns.
- Congestion: No weaving; first 2 km are allowed to be slow.
- GI issue: Skip/slow intake, sip water, reduce effort; never force a gel.
- Knee/foot/calf: 0-2 stable: monitor. ~3 or rising: back off. >3, progressive, or gait change: stop racing and consider stopping.
- Poor sleep: Do not panic; preserve breakfast and pacing. Use B/C if readiness and warm-up both feel poor.

## Post-race and marathon bridge

- **First 72 hours:** Walk gently, eat and hydrate normally, sleep, and record knee/foot/calf response. No running workout. Seek care for swelling, sharp focal pain, altered gait, or worsening symptoms.
- **Days 4-7:** If walking/stairs are normal and soreness is resolving: 1-2 easy 20-30 min spins and/or one 20-30 min easy run late in the week. Upper strength may return; no hard lower lifting.
- **Week 2:** If fully green: 3-4 easy runs totaling roughly 50-65% of pre-taper normal volume; two light strength sessions; no threshold until normal mechanics and recovery are re-established.
- **Winter-base entry criteria:** No recurrence after the race; normal walking/stairs; three symptom-stable easy runs including 60 min; lower strength tolerated; resting/recovery trends near baseline; motivation normal. Then build consistency before marathon-specific volume.
- **Spring target verified:** The official BQ.2 site currently lists the 2027 Spring Chance BQ.2 for Sunday, April 11, 2027 [17]. Baystate is a bridge, not proof by time alone: the strongest signal is performance plus a healthy race, healthy recovery, and resumed consistent training.
